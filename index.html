Underlying Theory

The Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT) bridges the microscopic world of moving particles with macroscopic properties like Pressure (P), Volume (V), and Temperature (T).

Deriving the Ideal Gas Law

According to KMT, pressure results from the continuous force of molecular collisions against a container's walls. The fundamental KMT equation for pressure is:

P = (N·m·vrms2) / (3V)

Where N is the number of molecules, m is molecular mass, and vrms is the root-mean-square speed. By recognizing that the average kinetic energy of a gas is proportional to its absolute temperature (Ek ∝ T), physicists combined this with empirical gas laws to derive the Ideal Gas Law:

P·V = n·R·T

This shows that Pressure and Volume are inversely proportional (Boyle's Law), and Volume and Temperature are directly proportional (Charles's Law).

Molecular Speed

KMT correctly predicts that not all molecules move at the same speed. The speeds follow a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution (shown in the top graph). The mathematical average of these squared speeds gives us the Root-Mean-Square (RMS) speed:

vrms = √(3·R·T / M)

This powerful equation proves two critical rules visible in this simulation:

  • Temperature Dependency: vrms is directly proportional to √T. Heating the gas causes the particles to move faster.
  • Mass Dependency: vrms is inversely proportional to √M. Lighter gases (like Hydrogen, M=2) travel much faster than heavier gases (like Oxygen, M=32) at the exact same temperature.

Simulation Scaling Note

In this simulation, each rendered "particle" does not represent a single atom; instead, it represents a massive "parcel" of gas. The code uses a pseudo-gas constant and an arbitrary mass multiplier so that when you select N = 150, V = 100, and T = 300, for example, the pressure is scaled up to be a highly readable ~1.0 atm.

Controls & Visualizations

Visualizations: The central view displays the gas sample encased within faint, transparent walls to clearly define the volume boundaries. The bottom is in contact with a heat bath. Red arrows (↑ / ↓) will flash to visualize heat flowing into or out of the gas.

Thermodynamic Processes:

  • Isothermal: Expanding or compressing the gas while maintaining a constant temperature (ΔT = 0). Heat is actively transferred with the bottom bath to stabilize the kinetic energy.
  • Adiabatic: Expanding or compressing the gas perfectly insulated from the surroundings (Q = 0). Compressing the gas does work on it, significantly raising its temperature, while expansion lowers it.

Simulation Units:

  • N: Number of discrete simulated particles (scaled macro-states).
  • M: Molar Mass of the gas, displayed in grams per mole (g/mol).
  • T: Absolute Temperature, displayed in Kelvin (K).
  • V: Piston volume, displayed in Liters (L).
  • P: Calculated internal gas pressure, displayed in Atmospheres (atm).
  • Speed: Translates simulated velocities to real-world Meters per Second (m/s).
  • Energy: Translates microscopic total kinetic energy to standard Joules (J).
