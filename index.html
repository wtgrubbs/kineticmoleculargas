Spherical Polar Coordinates & Wavefunctions

To solve the Schrödinger equation cleanly for an atom, we separate the 3D space into Spherical Polar Coordinates ($r, \theta, \phi$) because the central force of the nucleus is spherically symmetric.

The mathematical representation of an atomic state is a product of a Radial Function $R(r)$ and an Angular Function $Y(\theta, \phi)$:

$$ \Psi(r, \theta, \phi) = R(r) Y(\theta, \phi) $$
$N_{n,l}$ (Normalization Constant): Ensures the total probability of finding the electron anywhere in space sums to exactly 1 (100%). It is computed directly from the quantum numbers and physical constants:
$$ N_{n,l} = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{2Z}{n a_0} \right)^3 \frac{(n-l-1)!}{2n(n+l)!} } $$
$a_0$ (Bohr Radius): The most probable distance between the nucleus and the electron in a Hydrogen atom's ground state ($a_0 \approx 0.0529 \text{ nm}$).
$$ a_0 = \frac{4 \pi \epsilon_0 \hbar^2}{m_e e^2} $$
Where $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space, $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant, $m_e$ is the electron rest mass, and $e$ is the elementary charge.
$e^{-Zr/na_0}$ (Exponential Decay Factor): Dictates how the probability of finding the electron decreases the further you move away from the nucleus, scaled by the nuclear charge $Z$ and principal energy level $n$.
Polynomials (Laguerre & Spherical Harmonics): The algebraic bracketed terms are Associated Laguerre polynomials which dictate the number of "radial nodes" (spherical dead zones of zero probability). The angular component ($Y_l^m$ or sines/cosines) shapes the orbital into the recognizable dumbbell, clover, or complex 3D shapes.

Energy & The Zeeman Effect

In a standard one-electron atom without an external field, the electronic energy ordinarily depends only on the principal quantum number ($n$). However, when an external magnetic field is applied to the atom, these energy levels split depending on the electron's angular momentum. This phenomenon is called the Zeeman Effect.

The magnetic field couples to both the orbital angular momentum ($m_l$) and the spin angular momentum ($m_s$) of the electron (often modeled via the anomalous Zeeman effect). The resulting shift in energy breaks the degeneracy of the states and is represented as:

$$ \Delta E = \mu_B B (m_l + g_s m_s) $$

Where $\mu_B$ is the Bohr magneton, $B$ is the magnetic field strength in Tesla, and $g_s \approx 2$ is the anomalous spin g-factor of the electron.
